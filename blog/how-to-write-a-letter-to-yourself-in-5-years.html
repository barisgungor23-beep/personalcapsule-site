How to Write a Letter to Yourself in 5 Years

PersonalCapsule · 4 min read

Five years is long enough that life can change in ways you can't predict — which is exactly what makes a five-year letter so interesting to reopen. Here's a simple way to write a letter to yourself in 5 years without overthinking it.

Why five years works well

One-year letters are mostly about goals and habits. Five-year letters are about identity. In five years, your job, home, relationships, or priorities may all look different. Writing to that more distant self is less about predictions and more about leaving a clear picture of who you are today.

What to include

You don't need all of these — pick the ones that feel honest.

A snapshot of now. Where you live, what your days look like, who's around you.

What matters most to you today. The values you'd be sad to lose.

A hope and a fear. One thing you're moving toward, one thing you're unsure about.

A few questions. Things only your future self can answer.

A simple structure

Try this four-part frame:

  • Today: "Right now, my life looks like…"
  • Me: "The things I care about most are…"
  • Hopes: "Over the next five years, I hope…"
  • Questions: "When you read this, did…?"

That's a complete letter. It can be five sentences or five paragraphs.

A short example

In five years you'll read this. Today I'm renting a small place, working a job I'm unsure about, and quietly hoping for something more meaningful. What I care about most is staying curious and keeping the people I love close. I hope you took a real risk at some point. Did it pay off? And are you still the kind of person I'd want to become?

When to reopen it

Set the reopen date for exactly five years out and then let it go. The magic of a five-year letter is forgetting the details, so resist the urge to reread it early.
